About me

Rhidian Williams

✦ ✧ ✦

I'm a data scientist and engineer based in York. I build the pipelines, warehouses and models that turn messy source systems into numbers people can act on — currently at Nexus Brands Group, a multi-brand consumer goods business, and before that at Companies House.

I came to data through physics. A PhD at York spent four years on multi-terabyte experimental datasets, where nobody accepts a number they can't trace back to the measurement. That habit turned out to transfer: most of my work now is the unglamorous half of analytics, making systems that disagree with each other agree. The same physical unit can carry several product codes, two warehouses and three different ideas of whether it's discontinued. Resolving that — and writing the answer down so it stays resolved — is worth more than any dashboard built on top of it.

The full detail — roles, responsibilities and the technical stack behind each — is on my CV.

What I actually do

The group sells physical products across a lot of channels at once — own-brand websites, marketplaces in several countries, wholesale, and third-party logistics. Every one of those systems has its own definition of what a product is, what it costs, and how many are left. None of them agree.

Pipelines

Scheduled Python jobs that land raw extracts and API pulls into a staging layer on a Linux host, then load them into the group warehouse. Flat-file drops, REST endpoints and an integration platform all feed in; the landing layer is columnar files, so a failed run can be replayed without hammering a source system.

Modelling

A SQL layer that turns those feeds into shared sales, stock, margin and catalogue tables. This is where the definitional work lives — reconciling identifiers between systems, deciding what counts as sellable stock, and handling the cases where a field that looks authoritative has quietly been dead for years.

Reporting

Views a buyer, brand manager or ops lead can read without a data person sitting beside them. The measure of success is that nobody has to ask me what a number means.

Day to day: SQL, Python, pandas, columnar storage, cron, Git, and a healthy suspicion of any figure that looks too round.
