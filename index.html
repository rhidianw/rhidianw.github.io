About me

I work with data at Nexus Brands Group, where I build the pipelines, warehouses and reporting that a group of consumer brands runs on — pulling together NetSuite, Amazon, Magento and a pile of third-party feeds into something people can actually make decisions from.

Most of my time goes on the unglamorous half of analytics: getting the numbers to agree with each other. Stock that pools by one key and sells by another, listings that belong to the wrong account, flags that nobody has touched in three years. The dashboard is the easy part.

What I actually do

Nexus Brands is a group of businesses that sell physical products across a lot of channels at once — their own websites, Amazon in several countries, wholesale, third-party logistics partners. Every one of those channels has its own idea of what a product is, what it costs, and how many are left.

My job is to make one version of that story. That means ETL pipelines that land raw exports and API pulls into a warehouse on a schedule, a modelling layer that resolves the conflicts between them, and reporting on top that a buyer or a brand manager can read without a data person sitting next to them.

The interesting problems are almost always definitional rather than technical. Does "in stock" mean the warehouse has it, or that a customer can buy it today? If the same physical unit is poolable across four product codes, which one gets credited with the sale? Getting those answers written down and consistently applied is worth more than any amount of clever SQL.

Mostly SQL, Python, parquet, and a healthy suspicion of any number that looks too round.
